## Staff Categories Overview

<Info>
**International Staff:** Professional (P-1 to P-5), Director (D-1, D-2), Assistant/Under-Secretary-General (ASG/USG), and Field Service (FS-1 to FS-7) categories who are internationally recruited.

**Local Staff:** General Service (GS), Security Service, Trades & Crafts, and related categories who are locally recruited.

**Note:** Field Service staff (FS-1 to FS-7) are considered international staff.
</Info>

## 6. Travel Allowances & Per Diem

| Benefit/Allowance | International | Local | Appointment Type |
|-------------------|---------------|-------|------------------|
| **Daily Subsistence Allowance (DSA)** | <span style={{color: '#22c55e'}}>✓</span> (for official travel, covers accommodation, meals, incidentals) | <span style={{color: '#22c55e'}}>✓</span> (for official travel, covers accommodation, meals, incidentals) | T / FT / C |
| **Mission Subsistence Allowance (MSA)** | <span style={{color: '#22c55e'}}>✓</span> (non-staff personnel) | <span style={{color: '#ef4444'}}>✗</span> | T / FT / C |
| **Terminal Expenses** | <span style={{color: '#22c55e'}}>✓</span> (covers transportation between airport and accommodation) | <span style={{color: '#22c55e'}}>✓</span> (covers transportation between airport and accommodation) | T / FT / C |
| **Travel Advances** | <span style={{color: '#22c55e'}}>✓</span> (100% of terminal expenses and DSA) | <span style={{color: '#22c55e'}}>✓</span> (100% of terminal expenses and DSA) | T / FT / C |

<Info>
**DSA (Daily Subsistence Allowance):** Paid to staff on official travel at rates established by ICSC. Covers accommodation, meals, and incidental expenses. Reduced if lodging/meals provided free by UN, government, or related institution.

**MSA (Mission Subsistence Allowance):** Is specifically for non-staff personnel (like government-provided uniformed personnel - police officers, military observers, military advisers, etc.) deployed to field missions, not for regular UN staff members.

**Family Members:** When spouse or dependent children travel at UN expense, staff receive additional DSA at half the standard rate for each family member.
</Info>

## Key Appointment Type Distinctions

<Warning>
### Temporary Appointments

Temporary appointments have **significant limitations** compared to fixed-term and continuing appointments:

- **No expectancy of renewal or conversion** to any other type of appointment
- **Limited annual leave commutation** on separation (maximum 18 working days vs. 60 for FT/C)
- **Not eligible** for education grants (regular or special)
- **Not eligible** for language allowance
- **Not eligible** for home leave or family visit travel
- **Not eligible** for installation grant, repatriation grant, or rental subsidy
- **Shorter notice periods** for termination
- **Limited mobility and relocation benefits**
- **Settling-in grant:** DSA portion only (no lump-sum portion)

</Warning>

<Info>
### Fixed-Term & Continuing Appointments

Staff with fixed-term or continuing appointments have access to the **full range of benefits** based on their category (international vs. local) and duty station conditions.

</Info>

## Practical Examples

### Example 1: International Professional Staff (P-4, Fixed-Term)
**Eligible for:**
- Base salary + post adjustment
- Dependent spouse/child allowances (if applicable)
- Education grant (if serving outside home country)
- Home leave travel (every 2 years)
- Mobility incentive
- Hardship allowance (if at designated duty station)
- DSA/MSA for official travel
- Repatriation grant (upon separation)
- Language allowance: **Not eligible** (P-4 is above FS-5)

### Example 2: Local General Service Staff (GS-6, Fixed-Term)
**Eligible for:**
- Base salary + post adjustment (locally determined)
- Dependency allowances (if local conditions warrant)
- Language allowance (if proficient in 2+ UN languages)
- Special education grant for child with disability
- Hardship allowance (if at designated duty station)
- DSA/MSA for official travel
- **Not eligible** for: Home leave, education grant (regular), repatriation grant, mobility incentive

### Example 3: International Field Service Staff (FS-4, Fixed-Term)
**Eligible for:**
- Base salary + post adjustment
- Dependent spouse/child allowances
- Education grant (if serving outside home country)
- Home leave travel (every 2 years)
- Mobility incentive
- Language allowance (FS-4 is eligible if proficient in 2+ UN languages)
- DSA/MSA for official travel
- Repatriation grant (upon separation)
- **Not eligible** for: N/A (eligible for full international benefits)

### Example 4: International Field Service Staff (FS-5, Temporary)
**Eligible for:**
- Base salary + post adjustment
- Initial travel on appointment
- Hardship allowance (if applicable)
- DSA/MSA for official travel
- Settling-in grant (DSA portion only)
- **Not eligible** for: Language allowance (temporary appointment), education grants, home leave, installation grant, repatriation grant, rental subsidy
- **Limited** annual leave commutation (max 18 days)

### Example 5: Local General Service Staff (GS-4, Temporary)
**Eligible for:**
- Base salary + post adjustment
- Dependency allowances (if local conditions warrant)
- Hardship allowance (if applicable)
- DSA/MSA for official travel
- **Not eligible** for: Language allowance, education grants, home leave, installation grant
- **Limited** annual leave commutation (max 18 days)

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can local staff ever receive the regular education grant?">
    Yes, but only if they are **internationally recruited** and serving outside their home country. The education grant is tied to recruitment status, not staff category. However, this is rare for General Service staff.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why don't all Professional and Field Service staff receive language allowance?">
    Multilingualism is considered an **inherent competency** for Professional staff and senior Field Service levels (FS-6 and FS-7). The language allowance is designed to incentivize language proficiency specifically for support staff categories (GS, Security Service, Trades & Crafts) and junior Field Service levels (FS-1 to FS-5).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Are Field Service staff considered international or local?">
    Field Service staff (FS-1 to FS-7) are considered **international staff** and receive international benefits including dependent spouse/child allowances, education grant, home leave, mobility incentive, and repatriation grant. However, FS-1 to FS-5 are conditionally eligible for language allowance.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between DSA and MSA?">
    **DSA (Daily Subsistence Allowance)** is the standard per diem paid for official travel to cover accommodation, meals, and incidentals at rates set by ICSC. **MSA (Mission Subsistence Allowance)** is a special DSA rate established by the Secretary-General specifically for staff assigned to field missions, conferences, or extended duty periods away from their official duty station. MSA typically applies to assignments of 3+ months to UN field missions or 6+ months elsewhere.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens to benefits when a temporary appointment is converted to fixed-term?">
    Upon conversion, the staff member becomes eligible for the full range of benefits associated with fixed-term appointments, including education grants, language allowance (if applicable), home leave, full settling-in grant (both DSA and lump-sum portions), and increased annual leave commutation limits.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Are dependency allowances the same at all duty stations for local staff?">
    No. Dependency allowances for General Service and related categories are **locally determined** based on conditions at each duty station and comparator employer practices. Some duty stations may not provide certain dependency allowances at all.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I receive both mobility incentive and hardship allowance?">
    Yes, if you are internationally recruited Professional/Field Service staff with a fixed-term or continuing appointment serving at a designated hardship duty station, you are eligible for both allowances simultaneously.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the difference between termination indemnity and repatriation grant?">
    **Termination indemnity** is compensation paid when a fixed-term appointment is terminated before its expiry date (available to both international and local staff). **Repatriation grant** is a separation benefit paid to internationally recruited staff based on years of service upon completion of service and repatriation.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
